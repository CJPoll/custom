# athena:discussion-queue

The user wants to clear the decisions that are waiting on *them* — deliberately,
not in a rush. Your job is to surface those items one at a time, give each the
context and the systemic framing it needs, and facilitate a real decision. This
is the counterpart to [[athena:run-autonomously]]: there the user is away and you
decide-and-record; here the user is present and every call is theirs to make.

## 1. Assemble the queue

Gather the items genuinely awaiting the user's decision — don't invent work, and
don't include things you can and should decide yourself. Sources:

- **This conversation's open loops** — parked threads, questions you asked that
  went unanswered, decisions you deferred, approvals you're holding for, and
  follow-ups you flagged ("still on you", "whenever you're ready").
- Any tracked list the user points at (a Notion view of tickets assigned to
  them / `Needs Attention`, an artifact, a doc).

Give a **brief one-line enumeration** of the queue first, so the user sees the
scope and can reorder or drop items — then work it **one at a time**.

## 2. For each item — one at a time

Do not advance to the next item until the current one is decided.

1. **Context.** State what the item is, *why* it's open, what decision is needed,
   and the history that produced it — enough that the user never has to
   reconstruct why it's on their plate.
2. **Systemic considerations.** Weigh it against the whole system, not in
   isolation: how it interacts with other open decisions and prior ones,
   precedent and consistency, second-order and downstream effects, what the
   choice commits you to later, and any reversibility/blast-radius asymmetry.
   This framing is the point of the skill — surface it every time.
3. **Recommend, don't just enumerate.** Give a genuine recommendation with its
   tradeoffs, not a bare menu. If you're genuinely unsure, say so and say why.
4. **Discuss and debate.** Answer questions, steelman alternatives, and follow
   the user's lead. A clarifying question or a deliberate pause beats a rushed
   close — never push for speed.
5. **Decide and act.** Once the user decides, record the decision and its
   rationale where it belongs (the ticket, the journal, memory, or the
   conversation), then either do the work or queue it — and say which.

## 3. Move on

Only then take the next item. Keep going until the queue is empty or the user
stops. At the end, recap the decisions made and anything newly queued.

## Guardrails

- **One at a time.** Resist dumping the whole list; the value is in giving each
  item its own deliberate moment.
- **Only the user's calls.** Filter out anything you can decide under your own
  judgment — surface those as "here's what I'm doing", not as a decision to make.
- **Deliberate over reflexive.** Depth and systemic framing are the whole ask;
  don't collapse an item into a yes/no when it deserves debate.
- **The user decides.** You inform, recommend, and debate; you do not decide for
  them (that's [[athena:run-autonomously]]'s job, and only when invoked).
